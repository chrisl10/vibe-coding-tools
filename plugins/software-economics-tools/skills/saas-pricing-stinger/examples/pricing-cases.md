# Fictional SaaS, AI and seat cases

All prices, costs, targets and customer descriptions here are hypothetical supplied assumptions dated 2026-10-07, in USD per month. No market study or buyer contact occurred. [Inputs](economics-input.json) were run through the existing selected source Unit Economics calculator; [outputs](economics-output.json) are captured executable evidence, not live billing results.

## Small SaaS account

Supplied seed: USD 30/account-month; USD 5 variable service cost including support, USD 300 monthly overhead, hypothetical payment fee 3% plus USD 0.30, zero failed payments/refunds for this exercise. One account contributes 30 − .90 − .30 − 5 = USD 23.80. Fixed overhead break-even is 13 account-months; this does not prove demand. The hypothetical owner asks for at least 70% contribution margin, and this base case has about 79.3% before fixed overhead.

Provisional recommendation: test the supplied USD 30 flat account plan privately with explicit user/storage/support limits, because this bounded baseline covers the supplied economic target and the unit is understandable. No customer willingness evidence exists; public launch pricing remains unvalidated. Counterevidence to seek: buyers use a free substitute, value differs across segments, or heavy users exceed the assumed USD 5 cost. Recommend the owner-designed value/price conversations before any authorized paid pilot.

## AI-heavy workload

The same USD 30 flat price with USD 32 service cost produces negative USD 3.20 contribution/account-month under the supplied fees. Fixed-cost break-even is impossible in this scenario; volume deepens the modeled loss. This supports stopping an unlimited flat offer, not announcing a new price.

Provisional recommendation: keep USD 30 as a capped private-test candidate only if Operating Cost can document an included allowance keeping total service cost within the base scenario; otherwise the amount is unresolved. Prepare a measured fixed-fee-plus-overage proposal after customer comprehension, meter accuracy and overage economics are supplied. Credits require a clear customer-facing usage exchange; purchased, included and promotional balances must not be double-counted. No currency-to-credit rate or expiry is invented.

## B2B seats

Supplied candidate: USD 12/seat-month, sold in this fictional case as ten-seat USD 120 account-month invoices. Service cost is USD 50/account-month including support; hypothetical payment fee per account is USD 3.90; contribution is USD 66.10/account-month and fixed-overhead break-even is five ten-seat account-months. Do not apply the USD 0.30 fixed processing fee once per seat when there is one account invoice. The existing-owner fixture models account transactions, preserving this distinction.

Provisional recommendation: privately test USD 12/seat-month with a ten-seat buyer and explicit seat definition, because participation is the proposed value unit and the supplied economics are positive. Evidence still needed: whether all licensed people benefit, who approves seats, how offboarding changes billing, and whether procurement prefers workspace pricing. It is not a real customer endorsement.

## Missing evidence

A founder supplies only "an AI app" and wants a market rate. Buyer, job, workload, currency and willingness are unknown. Recommendation: complete the worksheet and resolve segment-specific Competitive Research before naming an amount. If a founder supplies a seed price, retain it as a hypothetical experiment candidate with limits; never label cost-plus arithmetic a market-tested price.

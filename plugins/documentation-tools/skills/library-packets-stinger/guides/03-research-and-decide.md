# Research and decide

1. Use external research only within the user's agreed scope. Prefer native Firecrawl/EXA when requested or available. If a specifically required connector is unavailable, report that limitation and continue independent library work; obtain an answer before substituting the required approach.
2. Read official current pages and primary publications rather than relying on search snippets. Archive the body with URL, fetch date, source type, publication date if established and any extraction limit. Never present a retrieved page as an exact earlier snapshot without evidence.
3. For competition, cover the features, pricing units, actor rights and availability that decide the buyer's job. Gather enough pages for feature understanding; do not fetch an entire site by default. Record unknown price, plan conflict and partial capture honestly.
4. Open the competitive brief with an executive decision and exact claimable edges. For each edge, name the buyer, precise claim, supported subject facts, named peer overlap and important limit. Use the current subject's name, never carry facts from one product into another library.
5. Make gap findings actionable: existing rule or missing contract, proposed design, affected source, acceptance cases and recommended priority. Separate product/contract additions, integration checks and unmeasured outcome benefits. A repeated feature name or absent homepage mention cannot prove uniqueness.
6. For research foundations, connect each publication to a specific design choice and retain its method, limitations and relevance. A published benchmark does not prove the subject product's own success.

These decision rules are derived owner requirements W02-W05 and D01/D02. Source locators help verify attribution, not factual truth or market exclusivity.

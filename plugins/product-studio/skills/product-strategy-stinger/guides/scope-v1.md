# Scope version 1 and the NOT-in-v1 list

Fills section 7. Questions: Batch 6. Method: Shape Up's appetite and no-gos [raw/shapeup-write-the-pitch.html].

## Steps

1. **Set the appetite first.** Ask how much time or money v1 is worth. This is a limit, not an estimate. Shape Up: if we only care enough to spend two weeks, what solution fits? [raw/shapeup-write-the-pitch.html]
2. **Find the smallest complete job.** What is the least that lets one best-fit customer finish the job from section 3 once, end to end?
3. **Sort every idea raised so far** into "in v1" or "NOT in v1". Default to NOT in v1 unless the owner says it is needed for step 2.
4. **Write the NOT-in-v1 list out loud.** Each item gets a one-line reason ("waits until 20 paying customers ask"). Shape Up calls these no-gos [raw/shapeup-write-the-pitch.html]; Strategy Blocks also asks for explicit non-focus areas [raw/lenny-strategy-blocks.html].
5. **Name rabbit holes.** Parts that could eat the timeline, and how you will sidestep them (for example "no custom domains in v1") [raw/shapeup-write-the-pitch.html].
6. **Check against the bets and the riskiest assumption.** v1 should serve the bets. If the riskiest assumption can be tested before building, that test comes before v1 work.

## Prototype is not product

A working demo built with an AI tool is a way to learn, not something ready to sell. SVPG separates "build to learn" (prototypes in discovery) from "build to earn" (commercial quality) [raw/svpg-build-to-learn-vs-build-to-earn.html]. Real products need reliability, security, scale, compliance, and support [raw/svpg-prototypes-vs-products.html]. If the owner plans to ship a prototype to paying customers, record that as a decision with the risk spelled out.

## Done when

Appetite stated, an "in v1" list, a NOT-in-v1 list where each item has a reason, and at least one rabbit hole. Log the cut as a decision note.

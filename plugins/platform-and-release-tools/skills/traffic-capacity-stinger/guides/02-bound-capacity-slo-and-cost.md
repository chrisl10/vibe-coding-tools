# Bound capacity, SLO and cost
Sources: [MIT](https://github.com/legioncodeinc/the-wasp-nest-source/blob/main/packs/platform-and-release-tools/skills/traffic-capacity-stinger/references/research/raw/mit-queue.txt), [overload](https://github.com/legioncodeinc/the-wasp-nest-source/blob/main/packs/platform-and-release-tools/skills/traffic-capacity-stinger/references/research/raw/google-overload.txt), [SLOs](https://github.com/legioncodeinc/the-wasp-nest-source/blob/main/packs/platform-and-release-tools/skills/traffic-capacity-stinger/references/research/raw/google-slos.txt), [Vercel](https://github.com/legioncodeinc/the-wasp-nest-source/blob/main/packs/platform-and-release-tools/skills/traffic-capacity-stinger/references/research/raw/vercel-limits.txt). Use the [worked case](../examples/fictional-spike.md).

1. Adopt supplied journey SLI numerator/denominator, objective, percentile and UTC window only after recording the owner's policy. Count failures, timeouts and exclusions honestly. Allowed bad events = eligible events × (1 − objective); consumed budget counts actual bad eligible events. A short window cannot silently stand in for a monthly budget.
2. Build a dated constraints table per actual provider/plan/runtime/region: configured concurrency/duration/memory, file/socket descriptors, DB and pool connection caps, request/rate limits, queue retention and downstream quota. Retain documentation URL, fetched date/hash, actual configuration evidence and unresolved entitlement. Document ceilings constrain options; they do not establish measured performance.
3. Use L = lambda × W only for matched admitted population and residence-time boundary with stable arrivals/departures and finite means. Convert milliseconds to seconds. L is mean in-flight occupancy including waiting when W includes waiting. It is not a recommended cap, p95 occupancy, worker count or peak prediction. Growing backlog fails the steady-state interpretation. Do not derive an M/M/1 tail from these inputs.
4. Estimate burst backlog change with explicitly constant scenario rates: delta Q = (arrival − completion) × seconds, accounting for drops/retries separately. Drain time Q/(completion − ongoing arrival) is illustrative only when net completion is positive and rates stay constant. No positive net rate means no finite drain estimate. Capacity may change with queue depth, resource pressure, cache warmth or failures.
5. Trace low/base/high traffic mix to CPU seconds, provisioned memory time, requests, bandwidth, DB, queue and external meters. Keep per-operation cost and expensive/retry paths explicit. Unknown rate is unpriced, not zero; capture currency, region, contract, date, allowances/floors/credits and billing scope. Hand the packet to selected Operating Cost for invoice-aware modeling; do not guess or multiply serverless duration into the wrong CPU meter.
6. Report measured bottleneck, forecast assumptions, error-budget exposure, cost budget and uncertainty. Proposed spare capacity must reflect actual recovery evidence and downstream limits. No toy formula proves throughput, improved latency, autoscaling success or customer readiness.

# Readiness and roles

This is a learning/advisory contract. It is not a competency certification or a substitute for the selected specialists. Primary guidance is cited in the [digest](research/distilled-builder-readiness.md).

| Area | What the beginner should know or demonstrate | Evidence and missing prerequisite |
|---|---|---|
| Product | Who needs it and one job it helps them finish | Owner example or missing user/problem decision |
| Scope | One small useful behavior, what is deferred | Accepted goal; scope still a proposal until accepted |
| Files/tools | Find a file, describe an edit, read an error | Reported experience or actual supplied exercise; no installation |
| Source control | A branch/change/review/rollback in ordinary language | Understanding pending; real workflow belongs to Git/Repo owners |
| Data/access | Which information is stored and who may read/change it | Unknown boundaries block a live-user recommendation |
| Quality | Expected behavior, failure case and evidence | A passing screenshot alone does not prove all behavior |
| Operations | Who answers users, monitors failures and restores data | Named owner or unresolved paid-user readiness |
| Commercial | Budget/time availability and business uncertainty | Route pricing/estimation/market validation; no guessed totals |

## Who is responsible

The human product owner decides the user problem, scope, priorities, budget and action approvals. The learner should explain the behavior, inspect outcomes and flag uncertainty; they need not memorize every language feature before making progress. AI can explain, research or draft within authorized scope. A named implementation owner handles code; security/quality reviewers provide their actual evidence; an operator owns support/recovery. These can be one person wearing several roles, but the responsibilities still exist.

Use a small workshop analogy: the owner chooses what the workshop will make; a draft is a sketch; the representative slice is a sample that shows the important construction steps; review checks the sample before selling anything. Limit: software can copy and fail differently from a physical object, and this analogy does not establish a manufacturing process or safety guarantee.

## Expectations to state plainly

Expect to learn, revise unclear requirements, inspect errors, review generated code and test failures as well as success. Learning/debugging/testing/rework are real work. Paid users introduce support, identity/data/money correctness and recovery responsibilities. A local demo, store account, AI-generated app or green isolated check does not by itself establish production readiness.

Do not give a universal hours-to-app promise or tool expense. Daily hours and usable learning/review capacity are inputs for selected Estimation; cost inputs go to Pricing/Operating Cost. Ask before consequential actions. Source examples or AI suggestions cannot expand permission.

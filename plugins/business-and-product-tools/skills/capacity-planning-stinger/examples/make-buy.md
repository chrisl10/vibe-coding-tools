# Fictional four-week make/buy arithmetic

Every quantity below is constructed for explanation on 7 October 2026. No salary, supplier performance, review ratio or ramp benchmark is implied. Model: [capacity arithmetic](../references/model.md).

A team's allocated available time is 160 hours for four weeks after holidays and leave. Existing support takes 24 hours, existing review 16 and coordination 8. Owner-chosen reserve is 8, recorded separately: `160 - 24 - 16 - 8 - 8 = 104` hours. Demand totals 200 hours, including 160 hours of prioritized scope and 40 hours explicitly deferrable. Skills and dependencies are compatible with the example's acceptance reviewer; that would need real proof in a customer decision.

The hiring scenario assumes, solely for this example, that a person starts in week three with 64 allocated hours remaining, spends 16 of those onboarding, and causes 16 additional hours of current-team onboarding load. `104 - 16 + (64 - 16) = 136` usable hours. The original demand gap is 64; the prioritized 160-hour gap is 24. This is no prediction of real hiring time or individual productivity.

The bounded service proposal is assumed to remove 80 hours of the same internally estimated demand through clearly accepted deliverables with a compatible skill. It needs 16 additional hours of current-team review and 8 of handover, which were not included in the baseline. `104 - 16 - 8 + 80 = 160` hours. Original demand gap is 40. Reserving the original 8 hours still applies; the service did not erase internal ownership. Displaced demand effort is an explicit fictional equivalence for this scenario, not inferred from supplier billable hours. Fictional supplier quote: `80 × USD 100 + USD 400 transition = USD 8,400` for the four-week scope. Fictional hiring cash proposal: USD 12,000 for this horizon plus USD 2,000 recruitment = USD 14,000, with employment costs unknown. Do not call that complete cost, approve it, or compare later months as if these first-period costs recur.

One provisional recommendation: prepare the bounded 80-hour service scope and defer the named 40-hour demand, because it fits the 160-hour priority under the supplied review/skill assumptions and preserves reserve. It is a local proposed decision. No hiring commitment or service purchase occurs. It reverses if the quote expires, reviewer availability drops, source ownership/exit terms fail, or the demand is persistent enough that a calibrated longer-horizon hiring scenario becomes preferable. HR Payroll handles engagement/legal questions and Services Quoting handles commercial scope; unavailable owners block their dependent stages.

# Worked decision cases

All cases below are synthetic worked examples, not customer research or executed demand proof. Thresholds are invented project decisions for illustration, not recommendations or industry benchmarks.

## Weak evidence

Assumption: small agencies will change their invoicing workflow. Source: a dated public article describes a large agency-software market (CONTEXT). No offer exposure or customer observations exist (MISSING); enthusiastic founder expectations are HYPOTHESIS. Decision: CONTINUE discovery, limited to preparing an authorized interview protocol about actual recent invoicing problems. No build recommendation is made.

## Counterevidence

An authorized manual workflow test was declared before exposure: at least 3 of 6 eligible agency operators complete a second invoice workflow unaided in seven days. Supplied source records show 1 completion and 4 explicit preferences for the current tool; one person did not respond and remains in the declared denominator. All are OBSERVED or REPORTED according to source. Decision: PIVOT the offer toward the observed reconciliation problem, then declare a new test. Lowering the success threshold to 1 after viewing results would be exploratory, not confirmation of the original claim.

## Missing data

A landing-page plan defines a signup proxy, but the channel has not been authorized and no exposure logs exist. The record remains planned; zero observed exposures cannot be converted into a zero-percent demand result. Decision: CONTINUE discovery by obtaining the necessary channel decision and choosing a feasible test within the budget. Execution remains UNEXECUTED.

## Small test with favorable observations

Before a local authorized prototype session, the team declared that 4 of 5 eligible users would complete a specific scheduling task without help. Supplied records show 4 completions, one confusing error path, and no payment or repeat-use observations. Decision: CONTINUE only with a bounded repeat-use/viability test and recovery design. The usability observation passes its local criterion; it does not establish market demand, willingness to pay, frequency in the population, or product-market fit.

## Economic stop

A supplied concierge test shows interest, but actual recorded delivery takes 4 staff hours per customer while the owner's accepted cost/time boundary permits at most one. Decision: STOP the current concierge delivery option under this constraint. Reversing the decision requires evidence of a feasible delivery method or a separately accepted economic boundary; positive clicks do not erase the constraint.

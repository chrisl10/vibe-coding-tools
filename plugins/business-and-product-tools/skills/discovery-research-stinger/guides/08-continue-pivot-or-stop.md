# Make one scoped next decision

Grounding: [research distillation](https://github.com/legioncodeinc/the-wasp-nest-source/blob/main/packs/business-and-product-tools/skills/discovery-research-stinger/references/research/demand-decision/distillation.md). This guide synthesizes pre-build discovery evidence against declared assumptions. It does not perform shipped-product analytics, infer statistical significance, write a PRD, or authorize implementation.

1. Load the original test declaration, observation sources, assumption map, desired outcome, and current practical constraints. Check provenance, exposure/metric definitions, criteria changes, exclusions, and actual test status. A planned test has no observed result.
2. Grade each evidence item using [evidence grades](../references/evidence-grades.md). Grade describes provenance/relationship to the claim, not confidence probability. Keep a public market statistic separate from customer behavior, and customer statements separate from observed commitments or usage. Read the referenced material before citing it.
3. Record support and counterevidence with relevance to segment, behavior, proxy, cost, logistics, feasibility, and safety constraints. Record missing information explicitly. Compare recorded results to the prospectively declared criterion without relabeling exploratory changes as confirmation. Small or biased observations cannot establish population frequency.
4. Choose exactly one recommendation: CONTINUE, PIVOT, or STOP. State its scope. CONTINUE may mean only continue discovery with the next smallest test when evidence is missing or unexecuted; it must not imply permission to build. PIVOT changes a named offer, segment, channel, or delivery assumption and proposes a new test. STOP ends the current proposed option or experiment because a specified constraint or contradictory evidence makes further work inappropriate under current conditions; it need not condemn the whole market.
5. Explain the choice in a short evidence chain: declared claim, actual observations/counterobservations, practical constraints, uncertainty, decision, and what evidence could reverse it. Keep the decision advisory; the owner accepts the next commitment. Do not use a numerical demand score to replace judgment.
6. Write [decision record](../templates/discovery-decision.md) with one next action, accountable owner, bound on time/cost, any external-action approval required, acceptance evidence, and next review point. Never send recruitment/outreach or execute an experiment as a side effect of writing this record.
7. For a proposed first version, use the scope guide to hand off an evidence-linked discovery brief. If no assumption was actually tested, label it HYPOTHESIS and continue discovery rather than calling it a validated solution. Formal requirements and Accepted contract changes remain with Library and their existing approval process.

# Individual-project forecast worksheet

1. Outcome and acceptance: what must a person be able to do, and what checks prove it?
2. Experience: first project or prior comparable deliveries? Which stack, AI tools and unfamiliar integrations?
3. Scope: first usable version, explicitly deferred work, unresolved decisions.
4. Availability: N hours per working day; weekdays (Monday=0 through Sunday=6); start date. Record usable fraction and its basis. This fraction covers interruptions and nonproject time only; named learning/review/rework stays in effort to avoid double-counting.
5. Evidence: completed comparable work, measured owner hours and completion criteria. If absent, label each scenario hypothetical and select one representative slice to calibrate before forecasting the full project again.
6. Scenarios: engineering, learning, verification and rework hours, with a written reason for each low/high difference. Zero review/rework needs an explicit justification. Do not quietly remove it when AI assists.
7. External prerequisites: identity checks, accounts, store review, supplied assets and third-party access. Use calendar offsets from the same start date; state what can overlap. Unknown dates stay unresolved, not zero. Sequential chains must be converted into prerequisite-ready offsets or modeled separately.
8. Cash: one-time expenses, monthly tool/build infrastructure, usage costs per scheduled owner hour if meaningful, currency, quoted source/date or user-supplied assumptions. No provider writes or purchases.
9. Owner time: optional hourly valuation. Show it separately from cash outlay. Continuing postlaunch operations belong to the operating-cost owner.
10. Result: conditional range of scheduled owner hours, working days, calendar release dates, direct cash and owner-time valuation. No confidence percentage without calibrated historical data. Explain the constraint most likely to move the result and when to reforecast.
